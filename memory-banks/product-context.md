## The business and user perspective

### Target User
Men shopping for gifts for the women in their lives. They're not stupid — they're just outside their comfort zone. They care, they just don't know what to do about it.

### User Pain Points
1. **Decision paralysis** from too many generic options
2. **Fear of choosing wrong** — a bad gift feels worse than no gift
3. **Time pressure** — shopping happens under deadlines (birthday in 3 days, anniversary tomorrow)
4. **Generic fallbacks** — candles, flowers, gift cards feel lazy but safe
5. **Context clue decoding** — "she said she doesn't want anything" is never true

### How It Works (User Flow)

**Step 1: Describe Her**
- One text box. No forms, no dropdowns, no 20-question intake.
- Prompt: "Tell us about her."
- User writes as little or as much as they want (sentence to paragraph)
- System extracts: relationship, vibe, occasion, budget, interests, constraints
- If critical info missing (like budget), asks conversationally

**Step 2: Curated Recommendations**
- 3–5 gift recommendations (not 50)
- Each includes: what it is, why it fits *her specifically*, where to get it, price, delivery timing, gift-readiness

**Step 3: Refine or Commit (Phase 2)**
- Reject picks with a reason, ask for more like a specific one, or lock in

### What Makes a Good Recommendation
Bias toward gifts that are:
- **Specific enough to feel personal** — not "a journal" but "a linen-bound journal from a small Italian stationery brand"
- **Slightly surprising** — what she didn't know she wanted
- **Not available everywhere** — if she can grab it at Target, it doesn't feel special
- **Experiential when possible** — classes, subscriptions, experiences beat objects for "has everything" women
- **Presentation-ready** — flag gifts that arrive beautifully boxed vs. needing wrapping

### Tone & Voice
- Confident, not salesy
- Brief, not verbose
- Honest about trade-offs ("this is beautiful but ships slow — risky if her birthday is Friday")
- Zero condescension
- Light humor welcome, never at the user's expense

### UI & Experience Philosophy
The experience must feel **calming and reassuring**. The user is likely already stressed.
- **Warm, not corporate** — feels like texting a friend who's great at gifts
- **Unhurried pacing** — no walls of text, no overwhelming grids
- **Conversational tone** — the app talks *to* the user, not *at* them
- **Confidence without pressure** — "here's what we think" not "BUY NOW"
- **Minimal chrome** — the text box is the product

### Visual Direction
- **"Whiskey Lounge" dark warm theme** — dark charcoal backgrounds (#1a1816) with warm undertones
- Copper accent (#c8956c) for CTAs, highlights, and focus rings
- Warm off-white text (#f0ebe5) on dark surfaces — product images pop against dark cards
- Glass-effect cards (white/5 with backdrop-blur)
- Generous whitespace (dark space)
- Rounded, approachable typography
- Subtle micro-animations (fade-in, pulsing ring loader, recommendations appearing one at a time)
- No stock photos of smiling women holding gift boxes

### Future Vision: Recipient Agents (Phase 3+)
Each woman in the user's life gets a persistent agent that accumulates context:
- What she liked (confirmed after the occasion)
- What flopped
- How her interests shift
- What's already been gifted (no repeats)
- Upcoming occasions

By the third gift, the agent knows her better than any generic engine. The user comes back because starting over elsewhere means losing that context.
